# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: DERMATOLOGY AND CUTANEOUS DISORDERS

## SECTION 1: ACUTE DERMATOLOGIC EMERGENCIES AND RED FLAGS
Acute widespread skin eruptions require immediate triage to rule out life-threatening dermatologic emergencies:
- Red Flag Conditions: Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) / Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), necrotizing fasciitis, erythroderma (>90% BSA involvement), and generalized pustular psoriasis.
- Warning Signs: Positive Nikolsky sign (epidermal detachment with lateral pressure), mucosal ulcerations (eyes, mouth, genitalia), high spiking fever with targetoid skin lesions, intense skin pain out of proportion to exam, and rapid purpuric expansion.
- Triage Protocol: Any presentation with mucosal involvement and epidermal sloughing must be immediately routed to the Emergency Department / Burn Unit for airway stabilization, fluid resuscitation, and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) evaluation.

## SECTION 2: COMMON INFLAMMATORY DERMATOSES (ECZEMA AND PSORIASIS)
1. Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema):
- Clinical Features: Pruritic, erythematous, excoriated flexural plaques (antecubital and popliteal fossae) with xerosis and lichenification.
- First-Line Therapy: Frequent bland emollient application within 3 minutes of bathing; low-to-medium potency topical corticosteroids (e.g., Hydrocortisone 1% or Triamcinolone 0.1% cream) for acute flares.
- Maintenance: Topical Calcineurin Inhibitors (Tacrolimus 0.03%-0.1% ointment) as steroid-sparing agents for sensitive facial and intertriginous areas.

2. Plaque Psoriasis:
- Clinical Features: Well-demarcated erythematous plaques with thick, micaceous, silvery scales on extensor surfaces (elbows, knees, scalp, lumbosacral).
- First-Line Therapy: High-potency topical corticosteroids (Clobetasol propionate 0.05% or Betamethasone dipropionate) combined with topical Vitamin D3 analogues (Calcipotriene) applied once to twice daily for 2-4 weeks.

## SECTION 3: ACNE VULGARIS AND CUTANEOUS INFECTIONS
1. Acne Vulgaris:
- Mild Comedonal: Topical retinoids (Tretinoin 0.025%-0.05% cream or Adapalene 0.1% gel) applied nightly to unblock microcomedones.
- Moderate Inflammatory: Topical Retinoid + Benzoyl Peroxide (BPO 2.5%-5%) + Topical Antibiotic (Clindamycin 1% gel); add oral Doxycycline 100mg daily for refractory papulopustular lesions.

2. Superficial Fungal Infections (Tinea Corporis, Cruris, Pedis):
- Clinical Presentation: Annular scaly plaques with central clearing and active advancing erythematous borders.
- First-Line Therapy: Topical Allylamines or Azoles (Luliconazole 1%, Terbinafine 1%, or Clotrimazole 1% cream) applied once to twice daily for 2-4 weeks. Avoid topical steroids which cause Tinea Incognito.
