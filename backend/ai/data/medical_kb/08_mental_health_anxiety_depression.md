# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: MENTAL HEALTH, ANXIETY AND DEPRESSION SCREENING

## SECTION 1: PSYCHIATRIC RED FLAGS AND CRISIS TRIAGE
Acute behavioral health presentations require rapid safety screening to prevent harm:
- Red Flag Symptoms: Active suicidal ideation with specific plan or intent, suicidal gestures, violent auditory hallucinations, command hallucinations, acute severe psychomotor agitation, catatonia, or severe delirium.
- Triage Protocol: Any patient expressing active suicidal intent, access to lethal means, or acute psychosis must be classified as an IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY. Initiate immediate psychiatric emergency evaluation or crisis line intervention (e.g. 988/Tele-MANAS/112). Do not leave the patient unmonitored.

## SECTION 2: STANDARDIZED SCREENING TOOLS (PHQ-9 AND GAD-7)
1. Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) for Depression:
- Score 0-4: Minimal or no depression. Monitor and provide lifestyle education.
- Score 5-9: Mild depression. Supportive psychotherapy and watchful waiting.
- Score 10-14: Moderate depression. Consider structured psychotherapy (CBT) and/or first-line pharmacotherapy.
- Score 15-19: Moderately severe depression. Pharmacotherapy plus active psychotherapy recommended.
- Score 20-27: Severe depression. Immediate pharmacotherapy initiation and mental health specialist referral.
- Item 9 Alert: Any positive response to thoughts of self-harm triggers mandatory suicide risk assessment regardless of total score.

2. Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item (GAD-7):
- Score 5-9: Mild anxiety. Stress management, sleep hygiene, and mindfulness.
- Score 10-14: Moderate anxiety. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and pharmacotherapy evaluation.
- Score 15-21: Severe anxiety. Structured CBT plus first-line SSRI/SNRI medication.

## SECTION 3: FIRST-LINE PHARMACOTHERAPY AND MONITORING
First-line agents for Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) and Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD):
1. Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs):
- Sertraline: 25-50 mg daily, titrated to 100-200 mg daily. Preferred in cardiovascular disease.
- Escitalopram: 10 mg daily, titrated to 20 mg daily. High 5-HT selectivity with minimal CYP interactions.
2. Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs):
- Duloxetine: 30-60 mg daily. Preferred when comorbid chronic pain or fibromyalgia is present.
- Venlafaxine XR: 37.5-75 mg daily, titrated to 150-225 mg daily. Monitor blood pressure at higher doses.
3. Clinical Counseling:
- Advise patients that therapeutic antidepressant benefits require 2 to 4 weeks to manifest, while mild GI side effects usually resolve within 7-10 days.
- Never discontinue abruptly due to antidepressant discontinuation syndrome.
