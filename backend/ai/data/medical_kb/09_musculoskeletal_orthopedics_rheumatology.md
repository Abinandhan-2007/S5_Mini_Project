# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: MUSCULOSKELETAL, ORTHOPEDICS AND RHEUMATOLOGY

## SECTION 1: ORTHOPEDIC AND RHEUMATOLOGIC RED FLAGS
Acute joint and spinal symptoms require rapid clinical triage to identify time-sensitive surgical emergencies:
- Red Flag Conditions: Cauda Equina Syndrome, Septic Arthritis, Acute Compartment Syndrome, and unreduced joint dislocations.
- Warning Signs for Cauda Equina: Acute saddle anesthesia (loss of sensation in perianal/perineal region), sudden bowel or bladder incontinence / urinary retention, bilateral lower extremity progressive motor weakness.
- Warning Signs for Septic Arthritis: Acute monoarticular swelling, severe erythema, warmth, inability to bear weight or tolerate passive joint motion, accompanied by systemic fever and chills.
- Triage Protocol: Cauda Equina Syndrome and Septic Arthritis require IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY evaluation with emergent spinal decompression or urgent arthrocentesis and IV antibiotic administration.

## SECTION 2: OSTEOARTHRITIS VS RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS DIFFERENTIATION
1. Osteoarthritis (Degenerative Joint Disease):
- Presentation: Morning joint stiffness lasting < 30 minutes, pain worsening with activity and relieved by rest; asymmetrical involvement of DIP (Heberden nodes), PIP (Bouchard nodes), weight-bearing knees, and hips.
- First-Line Therapy: Weight reduction, physical therapy, low-impact exercise; topical NSAIDs (Diclofenac gel) first-line for knee OA; oral Acetaminophen or short-course oral NSAIDs (with PPI gastroprotection) for flares.

2. Rheumatoid Arthritis (Systemic Autoimmune Disease):
- Presentation: Symmetrical polyarthritis with morning stiffness lasting > 60 minutes, pain improving with physical activity; classic involvement of MCP, PIP, and wrist joints with systemic malaise and positive RF/anti-CCP antibodies.
- Management: Early rheumatology referral; initiation of Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs (DMARDs) like Methotrexate 15-25mg weekly with Folic Acid co-prescription within 3 months of symptom onset.

## SECTION 3: ACUTE LOW BACK PAIN AND GOUT MANAGEMENT
1. Mechanical Acute Low Back Pain:
- First-line approach: Reassurance, maintaining light physical activity (avoiding prolonged bed rest), heat application, and short-term Paracetamol or NSAID therapy (up to 7-14 days).

2. Acute Gout Flare and Urate Lowering:
- Acute Flare: First-line Oral Colchicine (1.2 mg loading dose followed by 0.6 mg 1 hour later) or potent NSAIDs (Naproxen 500mg BID or Indomethacin 50mg TID). Avoid starting or changing allopurinol dosage during an acute attack.
- Chronic Management: Target serum uric acid < 6.0 mg/dL (< 360 umol/L) using Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors (Allopurinol 100-300mg daily) with Colchicine flare prophylaxis for the first 3-6 months.
