# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: PEDIATRIC COMMON CONDITIONS AND ACUTE CARE

## SECTION 1: PEDIATRIC EMERGENCY RED FLAGS AND TRIAGE
Infants and young children can deteriorate rapidly; immediate emergency triage is mandatory for:
- Red Flag Signs: Central cyanosis or SpO2 < 92%, grunting, severe subcostal/intercostal retractions, inspiratory stridor at rest (croup/epiglottitis), sunken fontanelle with dry mucous membranes and lethargy (severe dehydration), petechial/purpuric rash that does not blanch under glass pressure (meningococcemia), fever >= 38.0 C (100.4 F) in infants under 3 months of age, or seizures lasting > 5 minutes.
- Triage Protocol: Any child demonstrating lethargy, stridor at rest, non-blanching purpura, or age < 3 months with fever must be triaged as an IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY.

## SECTION 2: PEDIATRIC FEVER AND ANTIPYRETIC MANAGEMENT
Fever is a physiological immune response. The primary objective is improving child comfort, not normalizing temperature:
1. Weight-Based Antipyretic Dosing (Never use Aspirin in children due to Reye Syndrome risk):
- Paracetamol (Acetaminophen): 10-15 mg/kg per dose orally every 4-6 hours (maximum 60 mg/kg/day or 4 doses in 24 hours).
- Ibuprofen: 5-10 mg/kg per dose orally every 6-8 hours with food (maximum 40 mg/kg/day; only for infants > 6 months of age).
2. General Measures:
- Offer frequent oral fluids (breastmilk, water, diluted juices); dress child in light breathable clothing; avoid cold sponge baths or alcohol rubs.

## SECTION 3: ACUTE OTITIS MEDIA, GASTROENTERITIS AND ORS PROTOCOL
1. Acute Otitis Media (AOM):
- Diagnosis: Acute onset otalgia, fever, with a bulging, erythematous, hypomobile tympanic membrane.
- Antibiotic Strategy: Watchful waiting for 48-72 hours in children >= 2 years with mild unilateral AOM; first-line antibiotic: High-dose Amoxicillin (80-90 mg/kg/day divided BID) for 5-10 days.

2. Pediatric Acute Gastroenteritis & Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS):
- Dehydration Assessment: Mild-to-moderate dehydration (thirst, slightly dry lips, normal cap refill < 2s).
- WHO Reduced Osmolarity ORS Protocol: 50-100 mL/kg of ORS solution administered in small frequent sips (5-10 mL every 5 minutes using spoon or syringe) over 4 hours.
- Zinc Supplementation: 20 mg/day elemental zinc (10 mg/day for infants < 6 months) for 14 days reduces diarrhea severity and recurrence.
- Antidiarrheal Contraindication: Antimotility agents (Loperamide) are strictly contraindicated in children.
