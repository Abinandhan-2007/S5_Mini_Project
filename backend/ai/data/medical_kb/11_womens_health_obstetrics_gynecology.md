# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: WOMEN'S HEALTH, OBSTETRICS AND GYNECOLOGY

## SECTION 1: OBSTETRIC AND GYNECOLOGIC RED FLAGS
Obstetric and gynecologic complaints require rapid differentiation of life-threatening emergencies:
- Red Flag Conditions: Ectopic Pregnancy, Preeclampsia with Severe Features, Placental Abruption, Ruptured Ovarian Cyst, and Postpartum Hemorrhage.
- Warning Signs for Ectopic Pregnancy: Sudden sharp unilateral pelvic/lower abdominal pain, vaginal spotting/bleeding, and shoulder tip pain with positive pregnancy test in reproductive-age female.
- Warning Signs for Severe Preeclampsia: Blood pressure >= 160/110 mmHg after 20 weeks gestation, persistent severe frontal headache unresponsive to acetaminophen, visual disturbances (scotomata, blurring), epigastric/right upper quadrant pain, and rapid hyperreflexia.
- Triage Protocol: Any presentation suggestive of ruptured ectopic pregnancy or severe preeclampsia requires IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY hospitalization, urgent transvaginal ultrasound, IV magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis, and emergent obstetric evaluation.

## SECTION 2: ANTENATAL CARE PROTOCOL AND PRECONCEPTION SUPPLEMENTATION
1. Preconception and First Trimester:
- Folic Acid: 400 mcg (0.4 mg) daily starting at least 1 month prior to conception through 12 weeks gestation to prevent Neural Tube Defects (NTDs); high-risk patients (diabetic or prior NTD) require 4-5 mg daily.
- First Trimester Screening: Confirmation ultrasound at 6-8 weeks, baseline complete blood count, blood group & Rh factor, viral serologies (HIV, HBsAg, VDRL), urine culture, and fasting plasma glucose.

2. Second and Third Trimesters:
- Gestational Diabetes Screening: Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) 75g at 24-28 weeks gestation.
- Rh Immunoglobulin: Anti-D immunoglobulin (300 mcg) at 28 weeks gestation and within 72 hours of delivery for Rh-negative unsensitized mothers.

## SECTION 3: POLYCYSTIC OVARY SYNDROME (PCOS) AND ABNORMAL UTERINE BLEEDING
1. Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS - Rotterdam Criteria requires >= 2 of 3):
- Oligo- or anovulation (irregular, delayed menstrual cycles > 35 days).
- Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, severe acne, elevated free testosterone).
- Polycystic ovarian morphology on pelvic ultrasound (>= 20 follicles per ovary or ovarian volume >= 10 mL).
- First-Line Management: Lifestyle modification with 5-10% body weight loss; Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCs) for cycle regulation; Metformin for insulin resistance; Inositol supplementation.

2. Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (PALM-COEIN Classification):
- Acute heavy bleeding: High-dose oral progestins or Tranexamic Acid (1000mg TID during active menses) with pelvic imaging to rule out endometrial polyps or leiomyomas.
