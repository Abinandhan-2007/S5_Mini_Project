# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: ENT, UPPER RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS AND SINUSITIS

## SECTION 1: ENT EMERGENCY RED FLAGS AND AIRWAY THREATS
Upper airway and ENT emergencies require prompt recognition to prevent acute respiratory arrest:
- Red Flag Conditions: Acute Epiglottitis, Peritonsillar Abscess (Quinsy), Ludwig Angina, Retropharyngeal Abscess, and Mastoiditis.
- Warning Signs: Severe odynophagia, inability to swallow saliva with active drooling, muffled "hot potato" voice, trismus (inability to open mouth > 2 cm), anterior neck / submandibular induration, and toxic appearance with stridor.
- Triage Protocol: Any patient with drooling, stridor, or severe trismus must be triaged as an IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY. Secure the airway immediately; do not perform aggressive pharyngeal examination with tongue blade if epiglottitis is suspected.

## SECTION 2: ACUTE RHINOSINUSITIS: VIRAL VS BACTERIAL DIFFERENTIATION
Over 90% of acute rhinosinusitis cases are viral (rhinovirus, coronavirus, influenza) and do not require antibiotics:
1. Viral Rhinosinusitis (Common Cold):
- Symptoms: Nasal congestion, clear-to-mucopurulent rhinorrhea, mild facial pressure, sore throat, and cough lasting < 10 days without worsening.
- Symptomatic Management: Saline nasal irrigation, oral hydration, warm steam inhalation, oral Paracetamol/Ibuprofen for headache, and short-course topical nasal decongestants (Oxymetazoline 0.05% for maximum 3-5 days to prevent rhinitis medicamentosa).

2. Acute Bacterial Rhinosinusitis (ABRS - Clinical Criteria):
- "Persistent": Symptoms lasting >= 10 days without clinical improvement.
- "Severe": High fever (>= 39.0 C / 102 F) with purulent nasal discharge and severe facial pain for >= 3-4 consecutive days.
- "Double Sickening": Initial typical viral cold improving for 4-5 days followed by acute worsening with high fever, increased nasal discharge, and severe unilateral maxillary pain.
- First-Line Antibiotic Therapy: Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin 875/125mg BID or 500/125mg TID for 5-7 days in adults; 45-90 mg/kg/day in children). Doxycycline 100mg BID in penicillin-allergic adults.

## SECTION 3: PHARYNGITIS AND MODIFIED CENTOR CRITERIA
Differentiating Group A Streptococcal (GAS) pharyngitis from viral sore throat to prevent acute rheumatic fever:
1. Modified Centor Criteria (Score 0-4):
- Tonsillar exudates (+1)
- Tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy (+1)
- History of fever > 38.0 C / 100.4 F (+1)
- Absence of cough (+1)
- Age: 3-14 years (+1), 15-44 years (0), >= 45 years (-1)

2. Action Algorithm:
- Score 0-1: Low risk (< 10%). No throat swab or antibiotics indicated.
- Score 2-3: Intermediate risk (15-30%). Perform Rapid Antigen Detection Test (RADT) or throat culture; treat only if positive.
- Score 4: High risk (50%). Rapid testing recommended; if confirmed, treat with Penicillin V (500mg BID x 10 days) or Amoxicillin (500mg BID x 10 days). Use Azithromycin (500mg day 1, then 250mg days 2-5) for severe penicillin allergy.
